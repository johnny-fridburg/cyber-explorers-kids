Mission 01 · Packet Post

How does a message travel?

A network connects devices. Messages can be split into packets, which carry data and addressing information.

💻A device starts it

Your computer or phone creates data to send.

🛣️The route matters

Different devices help data move inside and between networks.

🗄️A service answers

A server can receive a request and send a response.

Tap the next stop

Follow the arrows from the laptop to the server. This is one simplified route; real networks can have more steps.

✉️ Message waiting at laptop

Pip: “We’re at the laptop. Which stop comes next?”

What if the route breaks?

Imagine the router is unavailable. Could data reach a server on another network using this route? No. But a local device might still be reachable. A connection to Wi-Fi doesn’t prove every part of the journey works.

Draw another possible route. Label what you know and what you invented.

Mission 02 · Sharing Space

Who needs to know?

Privacy is about making thoughtful choices about information and its audience.

🌍Public space

A post can travel beyond the people you expected.

🤝Trusted circle

Some details have a purpose only for people you know and trust.

🔐Private information

Passwords and sign-in codes don’t belong in chats or posts.

Sort the fictional cards

Choose a sensible audience for this scenario. Think about purpose, not just whether a detail seems interesting.

Pick an audience and compare your reasoning.

Can a tiny detail reveal a bigger story?

A school name, uniform photo, and daily location can add up. Look at a made-up character’s post and ask what someone could learn by combining the details. Don’t investigate real people.

Ask permission before sharing someone else’s picture or information.

Mission 03 · Message Detective

A message can wear a disguise.

Phishing tries to trick people into giving information or taking an unsafe action. Slow down and check.

⏸️Pause

Urgency is a reason to check—not a reason to hurry.

🧭Verify another way

Use a known app or contact, rather than the message’s link.

🙋Ask for help

A trusted adult can help check a message and respond.

Does a neat-looking message mean it is safe?

No. Logos, spelling, and familiar names can be copied. A warning sign is a reason to investigate safely, not proof of who sent it. Don’t reply with secrets. Ask an adult and verify through a known route.

If you clicked or shared something, tell a trusted adult promptly. You don’t have to fix it alone.

Mission 04 · Shield Studio

One shield. Different layers.

Protecting an account takes more than one trick. Each layer helps with a different kind of problem.

🗝️Long and unique

Use a different password for each account. A password manager can help, with adult guidance.

📲Another sign-in check

Multi-factor authentication adds a check beyond the password. Keep codes private.

🧰Recovery ready

A trusted adult can help set up recovery safely. This helps you regain access; it doesn’t prevent every attack.

Why not reuse one great password?

If it is exposed on one service, other accounts using it can be at risk. “Unique” means different—not the same password with a tiny change. Never use a password shown in a lesson as your own.

With an adult, look for account security settings in a service you use. Read before changing anything.

Could an AI give a convincing but wrong answer?

Yes. An AI answer may sound confident while guessing. Ask it to identify which clue supports each claim, then check yourself. Here, a sign-in followed by a save doesn’t prove who made the change.

Possible next checks: version history, which account saved it, and whether the club planned an edit. Ask people kindly; don’t accuse someone based on a guess.
